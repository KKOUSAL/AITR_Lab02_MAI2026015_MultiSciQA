| **Review topic and key papers** | Multilingual robustness of LLMs for scientific literature analysis (my G0 survey). (1) Singh et al. 2025, *Indic QA Benchmark*, Findings NAACL, 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-naacl.141. (2) Jin et al. 2019, *PubMedQA*, EMNLP, 10.18653/v1/D19-1259. (4) Agrawal et al. 2024, *Translation Errors Significantly Impact Low-Resource Languages in Cross-Lingual Learning*, EACL. All VERIFIED at the primary page. |
| **Research gap** | ORIGINAL (Singh et al., VERIFIED): on general Indic QA, Translate-Test beats native multilingual LLMs for very low-resource languages (e.g., Odia, Punjabi), while native input is better for mid-resource Hindi/Marathi. My review (Sec. 5.2, 8) says TT "should be compared with native processing" for *scientific* text. INFERRED: no such comparison exists for scientific QA in Indic languages (one search; P04). Scientific evidence is number-dense: 98.4% of PubMedQA test contexts contain digits (VERIFIED by execution), which translation may corrupt (Agrawal et al.). |
| **Research question** | **RQ1.** On biomedical yes/no/maybe QA, how does Translate-Test compare with native processing, per language and resource tier? **RQ2.** How consistent are a model's answers on the same item across languages, measured against its own English answer? **RQ3 (depth).** Do token fertility and number loss in translation explain where native or TT fails? |
| **Hypotheses** | **H1** (from Singh et al.): TT > native on Odia and Punjabi. **H2** (from Singh et al.): native ≥ TT on Hindi and Bengali. **H3** (from Agrawal et al.): number-preservation rate after EN→X→EN is lower for Odia/Punjabi than for Hindi/Bengali. **Contradicting observations:** native ≥ TT on Odia/Punjabi for both models (H1 fails, a valid negative transfer result); TT > native on Hindi/Bengali (H2 fails). |
| **Study pathway** | **B. Controlled comparative study.** Fixed models, items, prompt and scoring; the independent variables are pipeline (native / TT) and language. |
| **Dataset or measurement setup** | PubMedQA PQA-L (repo `pubmedqa/pubmedqa` @1cbae8e, MIT, VERIFIED). **Test:** the official 500 items (`test_ground_truth.json`). **Dev:** the other 500, used only for prompt and format checks. Questions and contexts are translated EN→{hi, bn, or, pa} with IndicTrans2-dist-200M (MIT, gated, VERIFIED); TT back-translates with IndicTrans2 indic-en. Translations are cached once and reused by every model. No training, so there is no train/test leakage; pretraining contamination of PubMedQA is acknowledged as a threat. |
| **Baseline/comparators** | (1) **Majority**: reproduced exactly at 55.20 acc / 23.71 macro-F1 vs the published 55.20/23.71 (VERIFIED by execution). (2) **English zero-shot LLM**: the validated baseline and upper reference; published references are Human 78.00/72.19 and BioBERT best 68.1 acc (VERIFIED, Table 5). (3) **Native-X** vs (4) **TT-X**, the comparators of interest. LLMs: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (VERIFIED, `qwen-research` licence) plus one more (UNKNOWN; to verify). |
| **Primary metric and control** | **Dependent variable: macro-F1** (accuracy secondary). RQ2: agreement rate and Cohen's κ against the model's English answer. RQ3: tokens per item ÷ English tokens (fertility); share of source numbers recovered after the round trip. **Held constant:** items, prompt template, label log-likelihood scoring (no sampling), seed, cached translations. **Decision rule:** per language × model, a paired bootstrap (10k resamples) 95% CI on Δmacro-F1 = TT − native; a difference counts only if the CI excludes 0. McNemar's test on per-item correctness, Holm-corrected over 8 comparisons. All results reported per language. |
| **Computing and risks** | Hardware ASSUMED: Colab T4 plus a local M1 (8 GB) for analysis, over 4 weeks (confirm). About 0.8M words of MT and about 9k scoring passes (INFERRED: hours, not days). **Risks:** (a) floor effect, so the G2 gate requires English macro-F1 ≫ 23.71 or a switch to Qwen2.5-7B 4-bit; (b) "native" inputs are machine-translated, which is the main validity threat, mitigated by RQ3 number checks and reported openly; (c) gated-model access, with NLLB-200 as fallback; (d) time, with a minimum of Hindi + Odia. |
